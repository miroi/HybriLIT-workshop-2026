# Exercise 02: Burning enthalpy of methane

## Gas-Phase Methane Combustion Reference

$$\text{CH}_4(\text{g}) + 2\text{O}_2(\text{g}) \longrightarrow \text{CO}_2(\text{g}) + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{g})$$

The standard gas-phase combustion enthalpy of methane (Lower Heating Value, LHV) at $298.15\text{ K}$ is **$-802.3\text{ kJ/mol}$** (**$-8.32\text{ eV}$**, or **$-191.8\text{ kcal/mol}$**).

#### Thermochemical Breakdown

Calculated from standard enthalpies of formation ($\Delta_f H^\circ_{298.15\text{ K}}$) via NIST data:

- **Methane ($\text{CH}_4$, g):** $-74.87\text{ kJ/mol}$
- **Oxygen ($\text{O}_2$, g):** $0.00\text{ kJ/mol}$
- **Carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$, g):** $-393.51\text{ kJ/mol}$
- **Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$, g):** $-241.83\text{ kJ/mol}$

Subtracting reactants from products gives:

$$\Delta_c H^\circ(298.15\text{ K}) = [-393.51 + 2(-241.83)] - [-74.87 + 2(0.00)] = -802.30\text{ kJ/mol}$$

## Note:
- **Standard LHV (Lower Heating Value) at $298.15\text{ K}$** is the standard enthalpy of complete combustion of a fuel when all water produced remains in the **gaseous (vapor) phase** at $298.15\text{ K}$ ($25\ ^\circ\text{C}$) and $1\text{ bar}$.
